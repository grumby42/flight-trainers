Archer ✈ Surfaces
Conceptual teaching model

This is a concept demonstrator, not a flight simulator. It illustrates relationships and cause-and-effect — which surfaces do what, why the wing stalls at an angle, how lift builds — using a simplified model with tuned, non-literal magnitudes.

Do not read exact speeds, angles, or rates as real-aircraft numbers. For performance figures use the POH; for procedures use the ACS and your AFM. Use this to explain the concept, then confirm specifics in the real references.

Stall speeds and flap notches follow the PA-28-181 POH (50 KIAS clean, 45 KIAS full flap; 0/10/25/40°). Build 2026-11-23T18:54:31Z · All trainers

Airflow overlays
1Torque — engine spins the prop right, so the airframe rolls left (Newton’s 3rd). Strongest at low speed + high power (takeoff).
2P-factor — at high AoA the descending (right) blade takes a bigger bite → more thrust on the right → yaw left. Strongest at high AoA + high power (climb).
3Spiraling slipstream — the corkscrew off the prop strikes the left side of the vertical tail → yaw left. Strongest at low speed + high power.
4Gyroscopic precession — the spinning prop is a gyro; a pitch input shows up 90° later in rotation. Raising the tail (taildragger) yaws left. Most relevant during pitch changes.
