Teaching notes — why the card lies

Cause (PHAK ch. 8): the Earth's field dips steeply into the ground at this latitude (about 68°). The float is weighted to keep the card level against that pull, so its centre of gravity is not at the pivot. Accelerate, decelerate or bank, and inertia or the vertical part of the field swings the card off the true magnetic heading.

ANDS (acceleration error): on an east or west heading, Accelerate and the compass shows a turn toward North; Decelerate and it shows a turn toward South. Largest on E/W, none on N/S.

UNOS (turning error): turning from a northerly heading the compass lags and may first show a turn the wrong way; from a southerly heading it leads. So you Undershoot North (roll out early) and Overshoot South (roll out late). The lead or lag is roughly your latitude, adjusted by your normal rollout lead. Largest near N/S, least near E/W.

Southern Hemisphere: both errors reverse. Drag the dip slider below zero to see it.

The other errors: variation (true vs magnetic north, about 5° W here), deviation (the aircraft's own magnetic fields, listed on the compass correction card) and oscillation (turbulence and rough control). Read the compass only in steady, wings-level, unaccelerated flight.

This is a teaching model: the directions and where the errors peak are right; the magnitudes are tuned for the classroom.
