Teaching notes — why the card lies

The cause: the float card is magnetized and the Earth's field dips steeply into the ground at our latitude (~68°). The card's center of gravity hangs slightly below its pivot. Any fore/aft acceleration or bank tips that offset CG, and because the card is also being pulled downward by the dipping field, it swings off heading.

ANDS (acceleration/deceleration): on an E or W heading, Accelerate → card shows North, Decelerate → South. Worst on E/W, zero on N/S.

UNOS (turning): rolling out of a turn, the card Undershoots North and Overshoots South. Worst near N/S headings, minimal near E/W.

Variation = true vs magnetic north (≈4–5° W near KEYE). Deviation = the aircraft's own metal/electrics; corrected on the compass card placard. Neither depends on motion — only dip does.

Pull exact field values for any airport from NOAA's WMM calculator (ngdc.noaa.gov/geomag) and set the dip slider to match.
