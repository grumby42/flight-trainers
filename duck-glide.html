      How to fly
      TiltHold the phone sideways like a yoke. Tilt left/right = bank. Tilt top toward you = nose up (slower), away = nose down (faster). Tap LEVEL to re-zero. RudderDrag the yellow RUDDER knob (springs back when you let go), or hold Z / X. The ball above it is the inclinometer — in a crosswind wing-low it sits toward the low wing, which is correct. SLIP = full rudder opposite your bank (a forward slip). Keys↑/W nose down · ↓/S nose up · ←/→ or A/D roll (bank holds) · L level wings · F / R flaps down / up · Z / X rudder · Space slip · [ ] trim · C camera · B wing-flap (Zen) · P pause GamepadLeft stick = yoke · A slip · LB/RB flaps · Y camera · Start pause TrimTrim sets the speed the duck holds with the phone at your LEVEL position (or keys released). It starts trimmed for best glide (Vg). Tilt = temporary change; trim = new "hands-off" speed. If you're holding the phone tilted to keep a speed, trim it out. Flaps change the trimmed speed (more lift at the same attitude), so re-trim after each notch — or leave Auto-trim on. LandingFlaps only once the pond is made; full flaps on final at the "Tgt" speed; round out ~25 ft, hold it off ~10 ft, touch near stall speed (~44 kt in the Mallard with full flaps). Bread trail🍞 Coins mark a glide path with margin. Collect them = right place, right energy. The PATH readout tells you high/low. Profile viewTap the minimap to flip between the top-down map and an approach-plate-style side profile: the bread-trail glidepath (dashed), terrain along the route, your duck, and your glide-reach line to the target. Green ringMinimap glide reach at best glide in the current configuration, shifted by wind. Target inside ring = you can make it. Flight-path markerThe magenta ⊖ is where you're actually going. Put it just short of the bullseye on final — that's your aim point. About the modelRead before teaching with it. (1) The Mallard is tuned to Cessna 172S POH numbers at 2,550 lb: Vg 68, Vs1 48, Vs0 40 KIAS, about 9:1 glide; the approach "Tgt" speeds are 68 / 65 / 63 / 61 KIAS. Speeds are shown as KIAS with no instrument or position error. The Cub and Goose are generic, not any specific aircraft. (2) The stall horn sounds about 5–6 kt above stall; real horns vary by airplane. (3) It is a point-mass model: no pitch dynamics, no flap balloon, no adverse yaw. Sideslip is real (a rudder input creates sideslip, side force and drag), so crab and wing-low techniques work. There is no yaw/roll coupling, and the gear has no cornering forces. (4) Water stands in for a runway; this is not seaplane technique. (5) Auto-trim is a game aid, and it is off for checkrides. (6) The engine-out restart checklist follows the C172S POH order. Its failure causes are simplified, and power after a restart is a fixed partial setting. (7) Wind is given in true degrees; heights are shown above the target, not MSL. Practice here is no substitute for dual instruction or the POH. PhysicsPoint-mass 3-DOF: real lift/drag polar (CD0 + k·CL²), stall at CLmax, ISA air density by altitude, ground effect, flaps & slip drag, boundary-layer wind, gusts, thermals and ridge lift/sink. Earth gravity on every world (even Planet Ember) so numbers transfer.
