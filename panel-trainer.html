Honesty grades — what's real vs. approximate

Turn coordinator + G-meter: direct accelerometer/gyro reads. The most truthful — no modeling. The ball slips for real when you corner.

Heading (DG): magnetometer, accurate. Unlike the compass it has no ANDS/UNOS — that's why you carry both.

Attitude (FPV): accurate at rest & slow tilt. Honest catch: it can't tell gravity from sustained acceleration, so accelerating reads a false pitch-up — the same physics that makes the compass lie under ANDS.

Baro altimeter + VSI: the S20 has a real barometer. Trend is real (drive uphill, ride an elevator); absolute value drifts and is noisy.

Airspeed: deliberately absent — a phone has no pitot/static. GPS groundspeed ≠ airspeed, and teaching them as equal is a dangerous habit.

How the altitude math works

Raw baro → MSL: your phone's barometer reads station pressure. The standard formula converts pressure to a pressure-altitude; applying the local altimeter setting (from the METAR, in inHg) shifts it to true MSL — exactly the Kollsman window on a real altimeter.

MSL → AGL: subtract the field/terrain elevation. We use a field-elevation slider since the phone has no terrain database.

Honesty: consumer barometers are noisy (a few feet to tens of feet) and drift. Trends are trustworthy; treat the absolute number as approximate.

Teaching notes — why the compass lies

Cause: the magnetized float card hangs from a pivot above its CG, and the field dips ~68° into the ground here. Fore/aft accel or bank tips that offset CG; the down-pulling field turns the tip into a heading swing.

ANDS: on E/W, Accel → shows N, Decel → S. Worst E/W, zero N/S.

UNOS: rolling out, Undershoot North, Overshoot South. Worst near N/S.

Variation ≈4–5° W near KEYE (true vs magnetic). Deviation = aircraft's own metal. Neither depends on motion — only dip does.
